a. Hydrologic cycle, water budgets, and the critical zone
i. Components and associated fluxes of the hydrologic cycle
Water basics
What is water
* H₂O → 2 H + 1 O
* O more electronegative than H → unequal electron sharing → polar molecule
* polarity → hydrogen bonding
* hydrogen bonds → major water properties
* water → excellent / universal solvent
* dissolves many ionic + polar substances
* hydrogen bonding → cohesion + adhesion
* cohesion → water molecules attracted to each other
* adhesion → water attracted to other surfaces
* capillary action → cohesion + adhesion → upward movement through narrow spaces
* surface tension → strong cohesion at air-water interface
Ice vs. liquid water
* solid water → H-bonded lattice → molecules held farther apart
* ice density < liquid-water density
* ice floats
* floating ice → surface insulation → liquid water beneath remains available for aquatic organisms
* ecological significance → aquatic organisms surviving beneath winter ice
* example → lakes freezing from surface downward rather than completely solidifying
Specific heat
* specific heat of water ≈ 4.184 J/g°C
* high specific heat → large energy input required for temperature change
* hydrogen bonding → energy absorbed before temperature rises substantially
* water → temperature moderator
* lakes/oceans → reduced temperature extremes
* climate regulation
* weather regulation
* coastal climates → smaller temperature extremes than interiors
* example → Chicago temperatures moderated somewhat by Lake Michigan
Phase changes
* evaporation → liquid → gas
* condensation → gas → liquid
* freezing → liquid → solid
* melting → solid → liquid
* sublimation → solid → gas
* deposition → gas → solid
* latent heat → energy absorbed/released during phase change without temperature change
* evaporation → energy absorption → cooling
* condensation → energy release → warming atmosphere

Freshwater distribution
* freshwater → low dissolved-salt concentration
* Earth water:
   * ≈97.5% → saline
   * ≈2.5% → freshwater
* freshwater distribution:
   * ≈68% → glaciers + ice caps
   * ≈30% → groundwater
   * very small fraction → lakes + rivers + wetlands + atmosphere + soil
* most freshwater → inaccessible for immediate human use
* accessible freshwater → highly limited
* constraints:

   * pollution
   * geographic distribution
   * seasonal availability
Salinity terminology
* salinity → dissolved salts
* freshwater → very low salinity
* brackish → freshwater + seawater mixture → intermediate salinity
* saline → high dissolved-salt concentration
* briny / brine → extremely saline water
* terminology thresholds → variable by source
* freshwater commonly → <0.5 ppt
* SciOly/wiki definition → freshwater <0.2% dissolved salts
* original classification:
   * briny → 50+ ppt
   * saline → ~30 ppt
   * brackish → ~0.5 ppt
   * freshwater → ~0 ppt
* key concept → salinity gradient rather than memorizing only one universal cutoff

Major flux definitions
* precipitation → atmospheric water → Earth's surface
* evaporation → liquid water → water vapor
* transpiration → water vapor released from plants through leaves/stems/flowers
* evapotranspiration → evaporation + transpiration
* infiltration → water entering soil from surface
* percolation → downward movement through soil/rock
* runoff → water flowing over land surface toward channels/lakes/oceans
* condensation → water vapor → liquid
* sublimation → ice/snow → vapor
* deposition → vapor → ice
* melting → ice → liquid
* freezing → liquid → ice
Water budgets
  

Definition
* water budget → accounting of all water entering + leaving a reservoir/system
* determines change in storage
* inputs → precipitation, inflow, groundwater recharge, etc.
* outputs → evaporation, transpiration, runoff, outflow, groundwater discharge, etc.
Basic equation
* Water inflow − water outflow = change in stored water
* ΔS = inputs − outputs
* ΔS > 0 → storage increase
* ΔS < 0 → storage decrease
* ΔS = 0 → steady state
Lake water budget
* ΔStorage = precipitation + surface inflow + groundwater inflow − evaporation − surface outflow − groundwater outflow
* example → Great Lakes
   * precipitation + tributary inflow + groundwater input
   * evaporation + St. Lawrence River outflow + groundwater loss
Watershed water budget
* precipitation → main input
* evapotranspiration → major output
* runoff → streamflow output
* infiltration → soil/groundwater storage
* groundwater discharge → stream/lake/ocean input
Residence time
  

Definition
* residence time → average time water molecule remains in reservoir
* long residence time → large reservoir and/or slow turnover
* short residence time → small reservoir and/or rapid turnover
Equation
* Residence time = reservoir storage / rate of outflow
* τ = S / F
Steady-state assumption
* same average inflow + outflow
* reservoir size approximately constant
* residence-time calculation → usually steady-state approximation

ii. Topographic and geographic controls on precipitation and climate
Orographic lift
   * moist air + mountain barrier
   * air forced upward
   * elevation ↑
   * pressure ↓
   * air expands
   * adiabatic cooling
   * saturation → condensation
   * precipitation → windward slope
   * descending air → warming + drying
   * leeward side → rain shadow
  

Adiabatic cooling
   * cooling due to pressure/elevation changes
   * no direct external heat transfer required
Diabatic cooling
   * cooling through direct heat exchange with surroundings
Rain shadow
   * windward side → wet
   * mountain crest → precipitation
   * leeward side → dry
   * example → Sierra Nevada → wet western slopes + Great Basin rain shadow
   * example → Cascades → western Washington wet, eastern Washington drier
Continentality
   * large continental interiors → far from major moisture sources
   * less atmospheric moisture
   * lower precipitation
   * greater seasonal temperature extremes
   * example → Gobi Desert
   * example → central Asia
Lake effect
   * cold dry air passes over relatively warmer lake
   * evaporation from lake → moisture added to atmosphere
   * moist air rises/cools
   * condensation → precipitation
   * strongest downwind
   * Great Lakes → lake-effect snow
  

Lake Michigan example
   * prevailing winds from west/northwest
   * moisture uptake over Lake Michigan
   * enhanced snow downwind → eastern/southeastern lake shore
   * Chicago region → lake-effect snow potential under favorable wind setups
Elevation
   * elevation ↑ → generally temperature ↓
   * temperature change → precipitation type + atmospheric moisture effects
   * high elevation → snowpack accumulation
   * snowpack → delayed water release

Rectangular

d. Fluvial sediment processes and channel form
i. Channel types and planform
Planform
   * channel geometry viewed from above
   * straight
   * meandering
   * braided
   * anastomosing
Straight channels
   * relatively low sinuosity
   * uncommon as truly straight natural channels over long distances
   * steep slopes / structural controls
   * resistant banks
   * artificial channelization → straightening
   * example → engineered urban river channels
Meandering channels
   * curving channel
   * broad floodplain
   * relatively low gradient
   * high sinuosity
   * erosion outer bend
   * deposition inner bend
   * common in mature/low-gradient systems
   * example → Mississippi River
Braided channels
   * multiple interwoven channels
   * wide
   * shallow
   * high sediment supply
   * sediment bars
   * channels divide + rejoin
   * unstable bars
   * sediment-rich rivers
   * steep-to-shallow gradient transition
   * glacial meltwater rivers
   * example → Platte River
   * example → braided rivers downstream of glaciers in Alaska
Anastomosing channels
   * multiple channels
   * divide + reconnect
   * relatively stable channels
   * vegetated/permanent islands
   * cohesive banks
   * lower channel mobility than braided systems
   * example → Saskatchewan River system
  

Sinuosity
   * measure of channel curvature
Sinuosity = channel length / straight-line valley length
   * straight → <1.1
   * sinuous → ~1.1–1.5
   * meandering → >1.5
   * higher sinuosity → more curvy
   * lower sinuosity → straighter

ii. Discharge, velocity, and load
Discharge
               * volumetric flow rate
               * volume/time

               * Q → discharge
               * A → cross-sectional area
               * V → mean velocity
  

Cross-sectional area
               * rectangular approximation:
               * A = width × depth
               * natural channel → irregular cross-section
               * multiple depth measurements → divided-area method
Velocity
               * distance/time
               * m/s
               * average velocity → not necessarily surface velocity
               * channel roughness + depth + slope → velocity controls
Water load vs. sediment load
               * water discharge → volume/time
               * sediment load → sediment transported/time
               * sediment:
               * bed load
               * suspended load
               * dissolved load
iii. Mathematical relationships describing open-channel flow
Manning equation
V = (1/n) R^(2/3) S^(1/2)
               * V → mean velocity
               * n → Manning roughness coefficient
               * R → hydraulic radius
               * S → channel slope / energy gradient
Discharge from Manning
Q = AV
therefore:
Q = (1/n) A R^(2/3) S^(1/2)
Hydraulic radius
R = A / P
               * A → cross-sectional flow area
               * P → wetted perimeter
Wetted perimeter
               * length of channel boundary in contact with water
               * excludes water surface
Wide-channel approximation
               * hydraulic radius ≈ depth
               * useful when width >> depth
Roughness coefficient
               * n ↑ → friction ↑
               * n ↑ → velocity ↓
               * n ↓ → smoother channel
               * n ↓ → velocity ↑
High roughness examples
               * boulders
               * vegetation
               * irregular bed
               * woody debris
Low roughness examples
               * smooth concrete
               * engineered channels
Chézy equation
V = C√(RS)
               * V → mean velocity
               * C → Chézy coefficient
               * R → hydraulic radius
               * S → slope
Hydraulic relationships
               * gradient ↑ → velocity ↑
               * hydraulic radius ↑ → friction relative to flow area ↓
               * roughness ↑ → velocity ↓
               * discharge ↑ → channel transport capacity ↑

               * saturated soils
               * urban runoff
               * impermeable surfaces
Flood frequency
               * frequency → how often a flood magnitude is statistically expected
               * recurrence interval / return period → average interval between events of specified magnitude
Recurrence interval
T = 1 / P
               * T → recurrence interval
               * P → annual exceedance probability
Examples
               * 100-year flood:
               * T = 100 years
               * annual exceedance probability = 1/100 = 0.01 = 1%
               * 50-year flood → 2%
               * 10-year flood → 10%
Important probability concept
               * 100-year flood ≠ once every 100 years
               * independent annual probabilities
               * multiple 100-year floods possible in same decade
               * no guarantee of one occurrence within 100 years
  

Probability over multiple years
P(at least one event in t years) = 1 − (1 − 1/T)^t
               * example → 100-year flood over 30 years:
               * 1 − (0.99)^30
               * ≈ 26%
               * example → 100-year flood over 100 years:
               * 1 − (0.99)^100
               * ≈ 63%
               * long-term probability ≠ schedule

### River dynamics / sediment


**River age + sediment load:** young river → high gradient + high stream power → more erosion + sediment transport; old/mature river → lower gradient + lower stream power → **less energy dedicated to sediment load** → more deposition


**Braided streams:** rapid/frequent discharge variation + abundant **coarse sediment** + erodible banks → multiple shallow channels + mid-channel bars/islands; high sediment supply + variable discharge; hard bedrock banks / fine sediment → less favorable


### Wetlands


**Marsh formation:** floodplain flooding + tidal influence in lowland/coastal areas + sediment accumulation; **NOT** typically large rainstorm events in forested areas


**Salinas:** human-modified wetland; **salt pans**; primarily **inland + arid environments** → evaporation → salt accumulation


### Deltas


**Birdfoot delta:** weak marine processes + heavy river discharge → long, narrow distributaries extending into water; high sediment supply; longshore currents can affect shape; gently curving coastlines = **NOT characteristic**


**Bifurcation:** single channel → splits into 2+ channels → **distributaries**; common in deltas, braided + anastomosing streams; opposite of tributary


**Bifurcation effects:** divides/redistributes flow → can limit flooding OR increase flooding depending on channel capacity/discharge; major distributor of **nutrients + mineral particulates/sediment**


### Groundwater / caves


**Air stripping:** water-treatment method → contaminated water + air → volatile contaminants transfer water → air; applicable to **petroleum byproducts**


**Aerated caves:** cave forms while water table high → later **water-table lowering** → cave sections above water table → air-filled/aerated


**Hydraulic conductivity (K):** ability of material to transmit water; **higher K → easier/faster water movement**; coarse gravel/sand → generally high K; clay → low K


### Critical Zone


**Critical Zone:** near-surface Earth system; interactions among **rock + soil + water + air + living organisms** → regulate life-sustaining resources; interconnected system


### Water properties


**Water:** H₂O → polar; **hydrogen bonding** between molecules; ice **less dense** than liquid water → floats; freshwater freezing point ~0°C; saltwater → lower freezing point


### Lakes


**Lake carbon:** lakes → generally **net CO₂ sources**; respiration + decomposition → CO₂ release; terrestrial organic carbon transported into lakes → processing + atmospheric CO₂ emission


**Freshwater lake zones:**
**Littoral** → shallow near-shore → light reaches bottom → rooted plants
**Limnetic** → open, well-lit surface water
**Profundal** → deep → little/no light
**Benthic** → bottom/substrate
**Benthos** → organisms living on/in bottom


### Thermokarst


**Thermokarst lakes:** permafrost thaw → ground ice melts → surface subsidence → depressions fill with water; climate warming/permafrost degradation; **terrain slumping + flooding + altered drainage/ecosystems**




### Groundwater / aquifers


**Water hardness:** mainly **Ca²⁺ + Mg²⁺** ions; groundwater through mineral-rich rocks → dissolves Ca/Mg → increased hardness


**Aquitard:** low-permeability layer → restricts groundwater flow; effective aquitard → clay/shale; **not effective** → highly permeable material such as coarse gravel/sand


**Unconfined aquifers — barrier islands + atolls:** water table open to atmospheric pressure; freshwater lens **floats above denser saltwater**; limited freshwater storage → vulnerable to saltwater intrusion/overpumping


### Remediation


**Ex-situ remediation:** contaminated material **removed/extracted from original location → treated elsewhere**; vs in-situ → treated in place


**Bioventing:** **Biological** remediation; air/oxygen supplied to contaminated soil → stimulates microorganisms → biodegradation of petroleum hydrocarbons


### Shoreline erosion


**Terrestrial forces → shoreline erosion:** rivers/stream runoff + sediment transport; groundwater; mass wasting/landslides; weathering; **NOT marine-only processes** such as waves/tides


### Stream development


**Downcutting:** vertical erosion → streambed elevation decreases; **upgraded** → increased erosional capacity / actively cutting downward; **not graded** → graded stream = balance between erosion + deposition


### Glacial floods


**Jökulhlaup:** catastrophic glacial outburst flood; subglacial volcanic eruption/heat → rapid ice melting → glacial lake fills → dam breach → sudden drainage/flooding


### Quick remediation classification


**Biological:** bioventing, bioremediation → microorganisms
**Physical:** excavation, air stripping
**Chemical:** chemical oxidation/treatment
**Thermal:** heating → volatilization/degradation
**Containment only:** caps, barriers, impermeable walls
**Ex-situ:** contaminated material removed → treatment elsewhere
**In-situ:** treatment occurs **at contamination site**
